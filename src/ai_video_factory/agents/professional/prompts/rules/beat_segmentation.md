# Professional beat definition and segmentation

## Definition

A beat is the shortest complete spoken-and-edit pulse that advances the viewer's attention, understanding, expectation, or feeling by one discernible step. It carries one immediate focus, one compact rhetorical or dramatic function, and one edit point at which that step can land on first hearing. Determine boundaries from the spoken rhythm and dramatic progression first, not merely from what is visible or from whether the image changes. A cut can create tension by interrupting a phrase before its payoff, then give the reveal its own beat and landing—even when both beats remain in the same scene or use closely related imagery.

Apply this definition throughout the complete script. Follow the narration at the scale of its successive attention steps: a shift in the action, evidence, claim, reaction, implication, contrast, question, emphasis, anticipation, or payoff gives the edit a new beat when it changes what the viewer is attending to or taking in. Within a sentence, split at a natural spoken emphasis when the first phrase sets up a question, expectation, or partial discovery and the following phrase supplies the answer or reveal. For example, “And the number is the same one” / “printed on the back of your debit card” should be two beats so the cut can hold back and then reveal the validating detail. Likewise, “You even pick up the card to check.” / “It matches.” should give the action and its confirmation separate beats. Do not keep these together just because they describe one continuous action or one visual subject. Keep each beat focused on one such step, with the source span, spoken emphasis, intended cut, and visual purpose aligned.

Pair every spoken pulse with a newly selected visual focus. Each successive beat advances the picture to a fresh view, detail, evidence element, action stage, visible state, or presenter-facing moment that makes that beat's attention step visible. A beat is both a spoken unit and a planned visual unit; its visual change can occur within the same continuing scene and preserve the scene's established people, objects, and spatial continuity.

The sequence of beats follows the narration's actual order of attention and meaning. Set boundaries at the moment a new step becomes perceptible, so successive beats create a legible progression of compact cuts and each thought lands cleanly on first hearing. Identify the spoken moments that invite anticipation, hold back information, sharpen a consequence, or deliver a reveal, and give each moment the cut and duration that make its tension and release audible in the edit.

## Cadence

The intro is the fastest-paced stretch of the video and should establish tension through its cutting rhythm. Give it sharply compact beats, placing boundaries at successive spoken emphases, changes in implication, and turns from anticipation to reveal so its opening movement produces a rapid, clear sequence of cuts. Let the narration's rhythm and intended rise or release of tension determine those boundaries before considering visual convenience; a single sentence can carry several successive pulses when its phrasing advances through anticipation toward a payoff. Use a brief setup beat, a cut, and a distinct reveal beat when that timing makes the audience wait for or better register the payoff. Do not make an opening beat long merely to keep one continuous visual together.

Carry concise beat sizing through the full script. Let every fresh focus, information unit, turn in meaning, spoken emphasis, or change in dramatic pressure register as its own pulse and edit opportunity, including developments inside a sentence. Use short source spans as the normal unit of progression; let a beat extend only as far as the single thought or action needs to become immediately intelligible. Do not use visual continuity, a shared subject, or a single grammatical sentence as a reason to suppress a cut that the spoken cadence or dramatic timing calls for. Vary the cadence through the function of each step: brisk cuts carry accumulating details and quick turns, while a consequential realization or instruction receives the space needed to register before the next step. Shape successive beats so setup, anticipation, reveal, and reaction each have a clear rhythmic landing, with shorter pulses increasing urgency and a measured hold giving a key payoff room to register. Maintain forward visual progression throughout, with the opening at the highest cadence and later sections continuing to cut on meaningful changes in narration and dramatic pressure.

## Source boundaries and exact wording

Read each complete source group before choosing boundaries. Follow the spoken progression and place each boundary where the narration advances to a new attention step or emphasis.

Every beat is an exact contiguous slice of its original source group. Preserve source wording, punctuation, casing, symbols, spaces, and their order. The ordered beat slices reconstruct the complete source group character for character. Python assigns sequential beat IDs and verifies source coverage.

Beat boundaries can fall within a sentence. Source grammar and spoken emphasis may carry meaning across neighboring beats; the surrounding sequence provides context for a purposeful phrase or fragment.

## Technical length limits

Every beat contains at most 100 decoded characters after stripping leading and trailing whitespace, and at most 40 whitespace-separated words. Choose source boundaries that preserve the spoken progression while satisfying both limits. The exact source text remains unchanged.

## Beat types

Choose one label for each beat according to its narrative pulse:

- orientation: establishes or resets time, place, situation, scale, or conditions.
- setup: prepares an immediate later beat while carrying its own narrative function.
- action: carries a physical, institutional, or procedural action or instruction.
- reaction: carries a person's, institution's, market's, or system's response.
- claim: states a substantive proposition.
- evidence: supplies source material supporting a proposition.
- data: centers a number, metric, date, valuation, count, duration, or calculation.
- causal_link: establishes how one condition produces another.
- consequence: states a result of an earlier action, condition, or mechanism.
- escalation: increases scale, stakes, reach, pressure, value, or severity.
- contrast: places states against each other so their difference changes meaning.
- analogy: maps one system or situation onto another to change understanding.
- revelation: introduces previously hidden or withheld information.
- pivot: redirects the governing direction, assumption, stakes, or question.
- correction: replaces or limits a prior belief or claim.
- question: opens or sustains an unresolved question.
- verdict: converts prior material into a conclusion, thesis, payoff, or punchline.
- return: brings back earlier material with altered meaning.
- transition: bridges into a new movement or future direction while carrying its own pulse.

The block-level types intro and close are group metadata. Beat types come from the 19 labels above.

## Joint audiovisual planning

Narrative boundaries and visual decisions are planned together. Each beat's visual carrier and shot purpose serve the attention step carried by its spoken pulse. Adjacent beats may share a continuing scene and preserve actor, object, state, and chronological continuity.
